# ADR-0002 — Marks have three states, not two

**Status:** accepted · 2026-08-02

## Context

The obvious model for a check is a boolean: it passed, or it failed. Every badge system works
this way, and it is one less state to design.

But a check can fail for two categorically different reasons:

- The thing being checked is genuinely not there — no README, repo is private, PR not merged.
- **Our check could not run** — the peer's host timed out, DNS failed, GitHub rate-limited us,
  a bot filter blocked our user agent.

A boolean collapses these into one rendering. On a site about thirty-two named peers, that
collapse means publishing "this person's site is down" when the truth is "our probe timed out."

## Decision

`MarkState = 'struck' | 'not-yet' | 'unknown'`.

- `struck` — the fact was verified.
- `not-yet` — the check ran and the fact was not true.
- `unknown` — **the check could not run.** Rendered as a dashed outline with a `?` glyph, and
  described in the accessible label as "not checked".

`unknown` is never rendered in the fault colour, never counted as a failure, and never sorted
below `not-yet`.

## Rejected, and why

**Boolean pass/fail** was rejected because it makes the product capable of libelling a peer. A
6-second timeout against a cold-starting free-tier deploy is not evidence of anything, and
publishing it as failure would be a false statement about a named individual who is also one of
our reviewers.

**Retry until certain** was considered and rejected: retries against thirty-two peers' hosts
from a build process is closer to a stampede than a check, and it would still terminate in
uncertainty eventually. Bounded concurrency (6) plus an honest third state is the correct
answer.

## Consequences — what we gave up

- **A messier UI.** Three states need three visual treatments, three aria descriptions, and a
  legend explaining the difference. A boolean badge needs none of that.
- **A weaker-looking headline.** "30 of 32 deploys responding" is less punchy than a green tick
  wall, and `unknown` marks make the roster look less complete than a competitor's.
- **More code, and a rule to hold.** Every check site must be careful to map "could not run" to
  `unknown`, and it is easy to regress that with a lazy `catch { return false }`.

## What would change our mind

Nothing about this product. If Hallmark only ever assayed our *own* work, a boolean would be
fine — the asymmetry only matters when the subject is someone else.
